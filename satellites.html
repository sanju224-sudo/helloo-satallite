🛰️ Indian Satellites

Discover the satellites that help India observe, communicate and explore.

🛰️

Aryabhata

Launch: 1975

Aryabhata was India's first satellite. It was named after the famous Indian mathematician and astronomer Aryabhata.

It was mainly built for scientific experiments in astronomy and space science.

📡

INSAT

Purpose: Communication & Weather

The INSAT system supports telecommunications, television broadcasting, weather observation and other services.

It became an important part of India's communication satellite infrastructure.

🌍

IRS

Full Form: Indian Remote Sensing

IRS satellites observe Earth's surface from space.

Their data can be used for agriculture, forests, water resources, mapping and disaster management.

🧭

NavIC

Purpose: Navigation

NavIC is India's regional satellite navigation system.

It provides positioning and timing information over India and parts of the surrounding region.

It can support transportation, navigation, emergency services and other applications.

🌧️

INSAT-3D

Purpose: Weather Observation

INSAT-3D is a meteorological satellite designed to provide weather-related observations.

It helps monitor clouds, atmospheric conditions, weather systems and related phenomena.

🔭

AstroSat

Launch: 2015

AstroSat is India's first dedicated multi-wavelength space observatory.

It observes astronomical objects in different wavelengths, helping scientists study the universe.

🌙

Chandrayaan Orbiter

Mission: Chandrayaan-2

The Chandrayaan-2 orbiter studies the Moon from orbit.

It carries scientific instruments that collect information about the lunar surface and environment.

☀️

Aditya-L1

Launch: 2023

Aditya-L1 is India's first space-based solar observatory.

It observes the Sun and studies solar activity, including the solar atmosphere and phenomena that can affect space weather.

🛰️ What Does a Satellite Do?

A satellite is a machine placed in space to collect information or provide services.

Satellites can help with communication, navigation, weather forecasting, Earth observation, scientific research and space exploration.

India operates and develops satellites for many of these purposes through its space programme.
