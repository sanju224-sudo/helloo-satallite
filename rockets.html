🚀 Indian Rockets

Explore the rockets that helped India reach space.

🚀

SLV-3

Full Form: Satellite Launch Vehicle-3

SLV-3 was India's first experimental satellite launch vehicle. Its successful launch in 1980 placed the Rohini satellite into orbit.

It was an important step in India's journey toward developing its own launch vehicles.

🚀

ASLV

Full Form: Augmented Satellite Launch Vehicle

ASLV was an experimental launch vehicle developed to improve India's satellite-launching capabilities.

It helped ISRO gain experience with more powerful and complex launch systems.

🚀

PSLV

Full Form: Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle

PSLV became one of India's most successful launch vehicles. It has launched many Earth-observation, scientific and commercial satellites.

It was also used for important missions such as Chandrayaan-1 and Mangalyaan.

🚀

GSLV

Full Form: Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle

GSLV was developed to launch heavier satellites into geosynchronous transfer orbit.

Later versions use an Indian cryogenic upper stage, giving the rocket greater capability.

🚀

LVM3

Earlier Name: GSLV Mk III

LVM3 is India's heavy-lift launch vehicle. It has been used for major missions including Chandrayaan-2 and Chandrayaan-3.

It is also an important launch vehicle for India's future human-spaceflight programme.

🛰️

PSLV & Commercial Launches

PSLV has also carried satellites for customers from different countries.

This has made India's launch services an important part of the global satellite-launch market.

👨‍🚀 Indians Who Went to Space

🇮🇳 Rakesh Sharma

Rakesh Sharma became the first Indian citizen to travel to space in 1984.

He travelled aboard the Soviet spacecraft Soyuz T-11 and spent about 8 days in space.

Important: Soyuz T-11 was a Soviet launch system, not an Indian rocket.

🇮🇳 Shubhanshu Shukla

Shubhanshu Shukla became the second Indian citizen to travel to space, flying on Axiom Mission 4 to the International Space Station in 2025.

His spacecraft was Crew Dragon, launched using a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket.

This mission was also not launched on an Indian rocket.
